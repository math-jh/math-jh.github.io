## Properties of Étale Algebras

We now define the notion of a separable extension. First, we will examine the properties of étale algebras discussed in the previous post.

::: Lemma 1
For an algebraically closed field $\mathbb{K}$, suppose that a commutative $\mathbb{K}$-algebra $A$ of finite degree satisfies $\Omega_{A/\mathbb{K}}=0$. Then in $A$, for any maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$, we have $\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}^2$.
:::
::: Proof
The field $A/\mathfrak{m}$ is a finite degree extension of the algebraically closed field $\mathbb{K}$, so it must be an algebraic extension, and therefore $[A/\mathfrak{m}:\mathbb{K}]=1$. Thus for each $a\in A$, to have $a-\lambda\in \mathfrak{m}$, we can find $\lambda\in \mathbb{K}$. Then the map

$$A \rightarrow \mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2;\qquad a\mapsto a-\lambda$$

defines a function $D:A \rightarrow \mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$ that is evidently a $\mathbb{K}$-derivation. Now from the universal property of [\[Multilinear Algebra\] §Differential Modules, ⁋Proposition 8](/en/math/multilinear_algebra/differential_modules#prop8){: data-lid="3fmpo" }, $D$ must factor through $\Omega_{A/\mathbb{K}}$, which is $0$ by assumption, so $D=0$.
:::

To establish our characterization of étale algebras, we need to show the following lemma, which starts from the result of the above lemma.

::: Lemma 2
For a commutative ring $A$, if a finitely generated ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ satisfies $\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}^2$, then there exists an idempotent $e\in A$ such that $\mathfrak{a}=Ae$.
:::
::: Proof
First, let the generators of $\mathfrak{a}$ be $a_1,\ldots,a_r$. Then, since $\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}^2$, for each $i$, the equation

$$a_i=\sum_{j=1}^r x_{ij}a_j$$

holds for some $x_{ij}$. For the resulting $r\times r$ matrix, let $I_r-(x_{ij})$ be $M$. Then, through the adjoint matrix of $M$, the equation

$$NM=(\det M) I_r$$

holds for some $r\times r$ matrix $N$. Now calculating where the column vector corresponding to $a_j$ goes on both sides, we know that $(\det M)a_j=0$ must hold for all $j$, and therefore $(\det M)\mathfrak{a}=0$ holds.

On the other hand, by definition the matrix $M$, after reduction modulo $\mathfrak{a}$, becomes $I_r$, so its determinant $\det M$ is also (modulo $\mathfrak{a}$) $1$. Now setting $e=1-\det M$, $e$ is the desired idempotent.
:::

Then the differential characterization of étale algebras is given as follows.

::: Theorem 3
For a finite degree commutative $\mathbb{K}$-algebra $A$, $A$ is étale if and only if $\Omega_{A/\mathbb{K}}=0$.
:::
::: Proof
First, since $\Omega_{A/\mathbb{K}}$ and the notion of an étale algebra behave well under base change, we may assume that $\mathbb{K}$ is algebraically closed. More precisely, $A$ being étale is equivalent to the $\overline{\mathbb{K}}$-algebra $A_{(\overline{\mathbb{K}})}=A\otimes_\mathbb{K}\overline{\mathbb{K}}$ being diagonalizable for the algebraic closure $\overline{\mathbb{K}}$ of $\mathbb{K}$ ([§Étale Algebras, ⁋Proposition 8](/en/math/field_theory/etale_algebras#prop8){: data-lid="eatek" }), while by the canonical isomorphism of [\[Multilinear Algebra\] §Differential Modules, ⁋Proposition 12](/en/math/multilinear_algebra/differential_modules#prop12){: data-lid="7ekiz" } and the definition of the $\overline{\mathbb{K}}$-algebra $A_{(\overline{\mathbb{K}})}$ we obtain the isomorphism

$$\Omega_{A_{(\overline{\mathbb{K}})}/\overline{\mathbb{K}}}\cong \Omega_{A/\mathbb{K}}\otimes_A A_{(\overline{\mathbb{K}})}=\Omega_{A/\mathbb{K}}\otimes_A A\otimes_\mathbb{K}\overline{\mathbb{K}}\cong \Omega_{A/\mathbb{K}}\otimes_\mathbb{K}\overline{\mathbb{K}}$$

and therefore $\Omega_{A/\mathbb{K}}=0$ is equivalent to $\Omega_{A_{(\overline{\mathbb{K}})}/\overline{\mathbb{K}}}=0$. That is, to prove the given theorem, it suffices to assume that $\mathbb{K}$ is algebraically closed and show that $A$ being diagonalizable is equivalent to $\Omega_{A/\mathbb{K}}=0$.

First, assuming that $A$ is diagonalizable, $A$ is generated by idempotents by the second condition of [§Étale Algebras, ⁋Proposition 6](/en/math/field_theory/etale_algebras#prop6){: data-lid="xi0j7" }. But for any idempotent $e$, considering the Leibniz rule,

$$\dd{(e)}=\dd{(e^2)}=e\dd{(e)}+e\dd{(e)}=2e\dd{(e)}$$

and multiplying both sides by $e$ gives $e\dd{(e)}=0$, so substituting this back into the above shows that $\dd{e}=0$. On the other hand, since $\Omega_{A/\mathbb{K}}$ is explicitly presented as the free module generated by such elements $\dd{e}$ modulo appropriate relations ([\[Multilinear Algebra\] §Differential Modules, ⁋Example 10](/en/math/multilinear_algebra/differential_modules#ex10){: data-lid="8jvjx" }), from this we see that $\Omega_{A/\mathbb{K}}=0$.

Now conversely, assume $\Omega_{A/\mathbb{K}}=0$ and let us show that $A$ is diagonalizable. We proceed by induction on the degree of $A$, and the case of degree $1$ is trivial. Now in the general case, choose a maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ of $A$. Then by [Lemma 1](#lem1){: data-lid="mpa5p" } we have $\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}^2$, and therefore applying [Lemma 2](#lem2){: data-lid="d57vz" } we can find an idempotent $e$ such that $\mathfrak{m}=Ae$. Meanwhile, from this, if we decompose $A$ into the direct sum of $\mathfrak{a}=(1-e)A$ and $\mathfrak{m}$, from the assumption that $\mathbb{K}$ is algebraically closed we see that the extension $A/\mathfrak{m}$ must have degree $1$. That is, we can write this direct sum as

$$A\cong \mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{m}\cong \mathbb{K}\times A/\mathfrak{a}$$

Now since $\Omega$ is a right exact functor ([\[Multilinear Algebra\] §Differential Modules, ⁋Proposition 13](/en/math/multilinear_algebra/differential_modules#prop13){: data-lid="649h0" }), $\Omega_{(A/\mathfrak{a})/\mathbb{K}}$ is a quotient of $\Omega_{A/\mathbb{K}}=0$ and hence is $0$. Therefore, $A/\mathfrak{a}$ is diagonalizable by the induction hypothesis, and so is $A$, which is its product with $\mathbb{K}$.
:::

Earlier, in [§Purely Inseparable Extensions, ⁋Example 9](/en/math/field_theory/purely_inseparable_extensions#ex9){: data-lid="ys0cs" }, we examined a situation that could cause problems when developing Galois theory; based on this example, let us examine [Theorem 3](#thm3){: data-lid="jhwvn" }.

::: Example 4
A favorable case, as we saw in the introduction to [§Purely Inseparable Extensions](/en/math/field_theory/purely_inseparable_extensions){: data-lid="ek8pv" }, is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q}$. From the computation in [\[Multilinear Algebra\] §Differential Modules, ⁋Example 10](/en/math/multilinear_algebra/differential_modules#ex10){: data-lid="vk5z0" }, we know that $\Omega_{\mathbb{Q}[\x]/\mathbb{Q}}$ is generated by $\dd{\x}$ as a free $\mathbb{Q}[\x]$-module. On the other hand, considering the ideal of $\mathbb{Q}[\x]$ given by $\mathfrak{I}=(\x^2-2)$, from [\[Multilinear Algebra\] §Differential Modules, ⁋Proposition 14](/en/math/multilinear_algebra/differential_modules#prop14){: data-lid="oenpk" } we have the following exact sequence

$$\mathfrak{I}/\mathfrak{I}^2\overset{\overline{d}}{\longrightarrow}\Omega_{\mathbb{Q}[\x]/\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}[\x]}\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})\overset{\Omega_0(u)}{\longrightarrow}\Omega_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow 0$$

from which we know that $\Omega_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q}}$ is generated by the element $\dd{(\sqrt{2})}$ as a $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$-module. But from the computation

$$0=\dd{(2)}=\dd{((\sqrt{2})^2)}=2\sqrt{2}\dd{(\sqrt{2})}$$

and the fact that $2\sqrt{2}$ is invertible in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$, we know that $\dd{(\sqrt{2})}=0$, and therefore $\Omega_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q}}=0$. 

On the other hand, for the algebraic extension of $\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{F}_p(t)$ given by $\mathbb{K}(t^{1/p})=\mathbb{K}[\x]/(\x^p-t)$ examined in [§Purely Inseparable Extensions, ⁋Example 9](/en/math/field_theory/purely_inseparable_extensions#ex9){: data-lid="yw03k" }, the computation above breaks down; just as in the computation above, $\Omega_{\mathbb{K}(t^{1/p})/\mathbb{K}}$ is generated by $\dd{(t^{1/p})}$ as a $\mathbb{K}(t^{1/p})$-module, but the computation

$$0=\dd{(t)}=\dd{((t^{1/p})^p)}=p(t^{1/p})^{p-1}\dd{(t^{1/p})}$$

does not yield the result $\dd{(t^{1/p})}=0$. Since $\mathbb{K}(t^{1/p})$ has characteristic $p$ anyway, $p(t^{1/p})^{p-1}$ is $0$, and thus the above equation gives no relation whatsoever on $\dd{(t^{1/p})}$. Indeed, explicitly considering any field $\mathbb{K}$ and algebraic extension $\mathbb{K}(\alpha)=\mathbb{K}[\x]/(f)$, we have

$$\Omega_{(\mathbb{K}[\x]/(f))/\mathbb{K}}\cong\frac{\Omega_{\mathbb{K}[\x]/\mathbb{K}}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\x]}\mathbb{K}(\alpha)}{\mathfrak{I}/\mathfrak{I}^2}\cong\frac{ {\mathbb{K}[\x]\dd{\x}}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\x]}\mathbb{K}[\x]/(f)}{(\dd{f})}\cong \frac{\mathbb{K}[\x]}{(f, f')}\dd{\x}$$

so the two computations above follow from this. 
:::

The case we wish to exclude is precisely when the minimal polynomial $f$ has a multiple root, that is, when $\dd{f}=0$; thus the notion of an étale algebra will be useful. We shall soon define a *separable extension* as a field extension in which every finite degree subextension is étale. ([Definition 8](#def8){: data-lid="pa54n" }) Then, as we saw in [Example 4](#ex4){: data-lid="16bod" }, any algebraic extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ is a separable extension. Furthermore, we will show that any algebraic extension of a perfect field is separable. ([Proposition 9](#prop9){: data-lid="4j2un" }) Let us examine a few more properties of étale algebras that we will use in this process.

::: Proposition 5
For any field $\mathbb{K}$, a finite degree, commutative $\mathbb{K}$-algebra $A$ is reduced if and only if there exist suitable finite degree field extensions of $\mathbb{K}$, $\mathbb{L}_1,\ldots, \mathbb{L}_n$, such that $A$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{L}_1\times\cdots\times \mathbb{L}_n$ as a $\mathbb{K}$-algebra.
:::
::: Proof
Since $\mathbb{L}_1\times\cdots\times \mathbb{L}_n$ is reduced, one direction is trivial. Conversely, let $A$ be a finite degree reduced commutative $\mathbb{K}$-algebra. If $A$ is a field there is nothing more to show, so it suffices to prove the case where $A$ is not a field, and as usual by induction on the degree of $A$ it is enough to show that any (non-field) reduced algebra $A$ can always be written as a nontrivial product $A_1\times A_2$.

To see this, we show that $A$ has an idempotent other than $0,1$. Since any ideal of $A$ is a finite-dimensional $\mathbb{K}$-vector space, among the ideals of $A$ other than $0$, we can choose an ideal of smallest dimension as a $\mathbb{K}$-vector space, $\mathfrak{a}$. Then by the minimality of $\mathfrak{a}$ and the assumption that $A$ is reduced, we have $\mathfrak{a}^2=\mathfrak{a}$, and therefore [Lemma 2](#lem2){: data-lid="p1o0m" } can be applied.
:::

Then the claim of [Proposition 9](#prop9){: data-lid="ppp3w" } mentioned above is essentially contained in the following lemma.

::: Lemma 6
For a perfect field $\mathbb{K}$, any finite degree, reduced $\mathbb{K}$-algebra is étale.
:::
::: Proof
Consider a $\mathbb{K}$-algebra $A$ satisfying the conditions of the claim. First, by [Proposition 5](#prop5){: data-lid="bufnc" }, there exist extensions such that $A\cong \mathbb{L}_1\times\cdots \times\mathbb{L}_n$. On the other hand, since the product of étale algebras is étale, for the given claim it suffices to show that any finite degree field extension over a perfect field $\mathbb{K}$ is étale. Therefore, applying [Theorem 3](#thm3){: data-lid="t28fx" }, it suffices to show that for any $\alpha\in A$, $\dd{\alpha}=0$. By the computation in [Example 4](#ex4){: data-lid="4u2cl" }, we know that the equation $f'(\alpha)\dd{\alpha}=0$ must hold, and by similar logic we must show that $f'(\alpha)\neq 0$.

Suppose for contradiction that $f'(\alpha)=0$. In any case, if $f$ is constant there is nothing to prove, so we may assume $f$ has degree at least one; then by the result of [§Fields, ⁋Proposition 19](/en/math/field_theory/fields#prop19){: data-lid="9rp1z" }, we have $\ch(\mathbb{K})=p\neq 0$ and $f\in \mathbb{K}[\x^p]=\mathbb{K}[\x]^p$. But $f$ is a minimal polynomial, hence irreducible, and an irreducible polynomial cannot belong to $\mathbb{K}[\x]^p$ by definition, which is a contradiction. Therefore $f'(\alpha)\neq 0$.
:::

Using the above lemma, we can now produce another characterization of étale algebras as follows.

::: Theorem 7
For any finite degree commutative $\mathbb{K}$-algebra $A$, the following are all equivalent.

1. $A$ is étale.
2. For any extension $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$, $A_{(\mathbb{L})}=\mathbb{L}\otimes_\mathbb{K}A$ is reduced.
3. There exists an extension $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$ such that $\mathbb{L}$ is perfect and $A_{(\mathbb{L})}$ is reduced.
:::
::: Proof
That the second condition implies the third is trivial from the existence of a perfect closure, and that the first condition implies the second is the result of [§Étale Algebras, ⁋Proposition 8](/en/math/field_theory/etale_algebras#prop8){: data-lid="bhl4b" }. Thus it suffices to show that the third condition implies the first. Now this third result follows again from [Lemma 6](#lem6){: data-lid="w3awf" } and [§Étale Algebras, ⁋Proposition 8](/en/math/field_theory/etale_algebras#prop8){: data-lid="t2se6" }.
:::

## Separable Extensions

The time has finally come to state the following definition.

::: Definition 8
An algebraic extension $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$ is called a *separable extension* if every finite degree subextension of $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$ is an étale $\mathbb{K}$-algebra.
:::

We can define this notion for non-algebraic field extensions as well, but for now we only consider separable algebraic extensions.

By definition, for any finite degree $\mathbb{K}$-algebra $\mathbb{L}$ that is itself a field, being étale and being separable are the same thing. From the definition, if $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$ is separable then any subextension is separable, and conversely if all (finite degree) subextensions are separable then $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$ is also separable; both are trivial.

The claim promised earlier can now also be easily shown.

::: Proposition 9
A field $\mathbb{K}$ is perfect if and only if every algebraic extension $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$ is separable.
:::
::: Proof
Since every finite degree subextension of any algebraic extension $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$ is a finite degree reduced $\mathbb{K}$-algebra, if $\mathbb{K}$ is perfect then one direction is trivial from [Lemma 6](#lem6){: data-lid="twy41" }. Thus it suffices to show the converse.

Suppose for contradiction that $\mathbb{K}$ is not perfect, and thus has characteristic $p\neq 0$. Then from the assumption that $\mathbb{K}$ is not perfect, we can consider a (relative in an algebraic closure $\overline{\mathbb{K}}/\mathbb{K}$) $p$-radical extension $\mathbb{K}(a)/\mathbb{K}$. On the other hand, from the embedding $\mathbb{K}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{K}}$, the resulting map $\mathbb{K}(a)\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{K}}$ is unique by [§Purely Inseparable Extensions, ⁋Proposition 6](/en/math/field_theory/purely_inseparable_extensions#prop6){: data-lid="4vdgp" }. In other words, the set $\Hom_{\Alg{\mathbb{K}}}(\mathbb{K}(a), \overline{\mathbb{K}})$ is a singleton, and therefore

$$1=[\mathbb{K}(a):\mathbb{K}]_s\lneq [\mathbb{K}(a):\mathbb{K}]=p^e$$

so $\mathbb{K}(a)$ is not étale and therefore not separable.
:::

The proof of the above proposition justifies calling $[\mathbb{K}(a):\mathbb{K}]_s$ the *separable degree*. On the other hand, even if $\mathbb{K}$ is not perfect, a particular polynomial $f$ may not have multiple roots. The following proposition is merely a compilation of what we have observed so far.

::: Proposition 10
For an irreducible polynomial $f\in \mathbb{K}[\x]$, the following are all equivalent.

1. $f'$ is not $0$.
2. $f$ and $f'$ are coprime in $\mathbb{K}[\x]$.
3. $f$ has a root in $\mathbb{L}[\x]$ that is not a multiple root for some extension $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$.
4. $f$ splits into a product of distinct linear factors in $\mathbb{L}[\x]$ for some extension $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$.
5. $f$ has no multiple roots in $\overline{\mathbb{K}}/\mathbb{K}$.
6. $\mathbb{K}[\x]/(f)$ is an étale $\mathbb{K}$-algebra.
7. $\mathbb{K}$ has characteristic $0$, or $\ch(\mathbb{K})=p$ and $f\not\in\mathbb{K}[\x^p]$.
:::

A polynomial $f$ satisfying these conditions is called a *separable polynomial*. Then restating [Proposition 9](#prop9){: data-lid="8dn5r" } once more, we see that $\mathbb{K}$ being perfect is equivalent to every irreducible polynomial in $\mathbb{K}[\x]$ being separable. Focusing on the element added through $f$, we can define as follows.

::: Definition 11
For a field extension $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$, an algebraic element $x\in \mathbb{L}$ is called a *separable element* if $\mathbb{K}(x)/\mathbb{K}$ is a separable extension.
:::

By definition, if $f$ is the minimal polynomial of $x$, then $f$ must be separable, and in this case $x$ is a simple root of $f$. These concepts all (of course) mean the same thing. That is, for an extension $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$, an element $x\in\mathbb{L}$, and the minimal polynomial $f$ of $x$, the following are all equivalent:

1. $x$ is separable.
2. $f$ is separable.
3. $x$ is a simple root of $f$.

Moreover, just as an extension in which every element is algebraic is an algebraic extension, the following holds.

::: Proposition 12
For an algebraic extension $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$, the following hold.

1. If $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$ is separable, then every element of $\mathbb{L}$ is separable.
2. Conversely, if $A$ is a set of separable elements of $\mathbb{L}$ and $\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{K}(A)$, then $\mathbb{L}$ is a separable extension.
:::
::: Proof
The first result is trivial, so it suffices to show the second. That is, we must take an arbitrary finite degree subextension $\mathbb{M}$ of $\mathbb{L}$ and show that it is étale. First, since $\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{K}(A)$ and $\mathbb{M}$ is a finite degree subextension of $\mathbb{L}$, we can choose finitely many elements $x_1,\ldots, x_m$ from $A$ such that

$$\mathbb{M}\subseteq \mathbb{K}(x_1,\ldots, x_m)=\mathbb{K}[x_1,\ldots, x_m]$$

Here, that each $\mathbb{K}[x_i]$ is a separable extension is trivial from the assumption on $A$, so they are étale; then $\mathbb{K}[x_1,\ldots, x_m]$ is obtained from their tensor product $\mathbb{K}[x_1]\otimes\cdots\otimes \mathbb{K}[x_m]$ by quotienting by the relations expressing their associativity and commutativity, and since $\mathbb{M}$ is a subalgebra of this, $\mathbb{M}$ is étale by [§Étale Algebras, ⁋Corollary 14](/en/math/field_theory/etale_algebras#cor14){: data-lid="ycpd2" }.
:::

Furthermore, a finite degree separable extension can be generated by a single element. That is, if $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$ is a finite degree separable extension, we can choose an appropriate $x\in \mathbb{L}$ such that $\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{K}[x]$. Such an element is called a *primitive element*.

[Theorem 14](#thm14){: data-lid="abfxf" } shows that a primitive element can always be found for finite degree separable extensions. For this, we need the following lemma.

::: Lemma 13
For an infinite field $\mathbb{K}$, fix a commutative $\mathbb{K}$-algebra $A$. If $A$ has only finitely many subalgebras, and $V$ is a subspace generating $A$, then there exists $x\in V$ such that $A=\mathbb{K}[x]$.
:::
::: Proof
Suppose that the subalgebras of $A$ other than $A$ itself are only $A_1,\ldots, A_n$. Then, first, since $V$ generates $A$, it cannot generate any of the $A_i$; that is, $V\not\subseteq A_i$. If we show from this that $V\not\subseteq A_1\cup\cdots\cup A_n$, then for $x\in V\setminus A_1\cup\cdots \cup A_n$, the subalgebra of $A$, $\mathbb{K}[x]$, cannot equal any $A_i$, and therefore we must have $A=\mathbb{K}[x]$, which will prove what is desired.

Suppose for contradiction that $V\subseteq A_1\cup\cdots\cup A_n$. From the given assumption, $V\not\subseteq A_n$, so there exists some $x\in V\setminus A_n$. Then for any $y\in V$, we can consider the infinite set

$$\{x\}\cup \{y+\lambda x\mid \lambda\in\mathbb{K}\}$$

This set is a subset of $V$ and therefore a subset of $A_1\cup\cdots\cup A_n$. Hence, by the pigeonhole principle, at least two of these must belong to the same $A_i$, and from this fact we know that $x,y$ are simultaneously contained in some $A_i$. By assumption, $x\not\in A_n$, so $i$ cannot be $n$, and therefore $y$ (just like $x$) must belong to one of $A_1,\ldots, A_{n-1}$. From this we see that $y$ must belong to $A_1\cup\cdots \cup A_{n-1}$, and since $y$ is an arbitrary element of $V$, we have $V\subseteq A_1\cup\cdots\cup A_{n-1}$.

Now, repeating this process inductively, we obtain $V\subseteq A_1$, which is a contradiction; therefore we must have $V\not\subseteq A_1\cup\cdots\cup A_n$.
:::

::: Theorem 14
Fix an infinite field $\mathbb{K}$. For an algebraic extension $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$, the following are equivalent.

1. $\mathbb{L}$ has a primitive element.
2. $\mathbb{L}$ has only finitely many subextensions.
:::
::: Proof
First, suppose $\mathbb{L}$ has a primitive element $x$, and let $f(\x)\in \mathbb{K}[\x]$ be the minimal polynomial of $x$. If we consider a monic polynomial dividing the polynomial $f(\x)$ in $\mathbb{L}[\x]$, namely $g(\x)\in \mathbb{L}[\x]$, then whenever such a polynomial $g$ is given, we can consider the subextension generated by the coefficients of $g$ in $\mathbb{L}$. Denote this by $\mathbb{K}_g$. Then our claim is that these $\mathbb{K}_g$ are precisely the subextensions of $\mathbb{L}$. In particular, if $f$ has degree $d$, there are at most $2^d$ such extensions, so the second condition will hold.

To prove the claim, let $\mathbb{M}$ be an arbitrary subextension. Then since $x$ is a primitive element, $\mathbb{M}[x]=\mathbb{L}$ holds. That is, $x$ is an algebraic element of the extension $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{M}$, so we can choose a minimal polynomial $h(\x)\in\mathbb{M}[\x]$, which by [§Algebraic Extensions, ⁋Theorem 15](/en/math/field_theory/algebraic_extensions#thm15){: data-lid="saiw9" } is a monic polynomial in $\mathbb{L}[\x]$ dividing $f$. Thus $\mathbb{K}_h$ is defined as above, and by its definition $\mathbb{K}_h\subseteq\mathbb{M}$. On the other hand, since $x$ is a primitive element of $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$, we have the equality

$$\mathbb{K}_h[x]=\mathbb{M}[x]=\mathbb{L}$$

But by definition $[\mathbb{L}:\mathbb{M}]=\deg h$, and since $h(\x)\in \mathbb{K}_h[\x]$ satisfies $h(x)=0$, we have $[\mathbb{L}:\mathbb{K}_h]\leq\deg h$. From this we conclude that $\mathbb{K}_h=\mathbb{M}$ must hold.

Now assuming the second condition, since $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$ has only finitely many subextensions, setting $A=\mathbb{L}$ satisfies the hypotheses of [Lemma 13](#lem13){: data-lid="tt88o" }, and we obtain the desired result.
:::

In particular, if $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$ is a finite degree separable extension, then it is in particular a finite degree étale $\mathbb{K}$-algebra, and therefore by [§Étale Algebras, ⁋Proposition 9](/en/math/field_theory/etale_algebras#prop9){: data-lid="m5tmf" } we know that the second condition of the above theorem holds.

[Theorem 14](#thm14){: data-lid="62gg5" } always holds even when $\mathbb{K}$ is a finite field, but to prove this requires a somewhat more delicate counting argument than [Lemma 13](#lem13){: data-lid="nbabs" }, so we postpone it.

On the other hand, separability is essentially (almost) an étale algebra, and since étale algebras behave well under base change ([§Étale Algebras, ⁋Corollary 14](/en/math/field_theory/etale_algebras#cor14){: data-lid="vwjxx" }), with slight modifications as in the proof of [Proposition 12](#prop12){: data-lid="vk83n" }, we can show that separability also behaves well under base change in the following two cases.

::: Proposition 15
For an algebraic extension $\mathbb{M}/\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$, $\mathbb{M}/\mathbb{K}$ is separable if and only if both $\mathbb{M}/\mathbb{L}$ and $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$ are separable.
:::
::: Proof
If $\mathbb{M}/\mathbb{K}$ is separable, then that its subextension $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$ is separable is clear from the definition, and since any element of $\mathbb{M}$ is separable over $\mathbb{K}$ ([Proposition 12](#prop12){: data-lid="2z70x" }), viewing it in the extension $\mathbb{M}/\mathbb{L}$, it is also separable over $\mathbb{L}$ ([Proposition 10](#prop10){: data-lid="548k5" }). That is, since every element of $\mathbb{M}$ is separable, we know again by [Proposition 12](#prop12){: data-lid="bckex" } that $\mathbb{M}/\mathbb{L}$ is separable.

Therefore, the core of this proposition is the converse. First, if $\mathbb{M}/\mathbb{K}$ had finite degree, then separable algebras and étale algebras are the same thing, so the claim is immediate from the third result of [§Étale Algebras, ⁋Proposition 12](/en/math/field_theory/etale_algebras#prop12){: data-lid="zamc1" }. The idea of the proof, similar to the above, is that even if $\mathbb{M}/\mathbb{K}$ has infinite degree, looking at a single specific element allows us to reduce this to finite degree.

Suppose an arbitrary $x\in \mathbb{M}$ is given, and its minimal polynomial $f\in\mathbb{L}[\x]$ is given; then $f$ is a separable polynomial. Now for $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$, define the subextension $\mathbb{L}'$ to be the subextension obtained by adjoining the coefficients appearing in $f$ to $\mathbb{K}$. Then since $\mathbb{L}'$ is generated by finitely many algebraic elements, $\mathbb{L}'/\mathbb{K}$ is a finite degree extension. Now $f$ is, in both $\mathbb{L}$ and $\mathbb{L}'$, the minimal polynomial of $x$, and since it is separable in $\mathbb{L}$, it is also separable in $\mathbb{L}'$. That is, $x$ is a separable element of the extension $\mathbb{M}/\mathbb{L}'$, and setting $\mathbb{M}'=\mathbb{L}'(x)$, $\mathbb{M}'/\mathbb{L}'$ is a finite degree separable extension, that is, a finite degree étale algebra. On the other hand, since $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$ is separable, its subextension $\mathbb{L}'$ is also separable, and therefore $\mathbb{L}'/\mathbb{K}$ is also a finite degree étale algebra. Hence their tower $\mathbb{M}'/\mathbb{K}$ is also a finite degree étale algebra, and therefore a finite degree separable extension. That is, $x$ is a separable element over $\mathbb{K}$, and since the choice of $x$ was arbitrary, we obtain the desired result.
:::

::: Proposition 16
Fix a suitable extension of $\mathbb{K}$, and suppose a subextension $\mathbb{K'}/\mathbb{K}$ and an algebraic subextension $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$ of this extension are given. Then for $\mathbb{L}'=\mathbb{K}'(\mathbb{L})$, the following hold.

1. If $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$ is separable, then so is $\mathbb{L}'/\mathbb{K}'$.
2. Conversely, if $\mathbb{L}'/\mathbb{K}'$ is separable and $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}, \mathbb{K}'/\mathbb{K}$ are linearly disjoint, then $\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}$ is also separable.
:::
::: Proof
1. By assumption, every element of $\mathbb{L}$ is separable over $\mathbb{K}$, so every element of $\mathbb{K}'(\mathbb{L})$ is separable over $\mathbb{K}'$.
2. To show this, we must show that any finite degree subextension $\mathbb{M}/\mathbb{K}$ is étale. First, from the given assumption, $\mathbb{M}$ and $\mathbb{K}'$ are linearly disjoint, and thus $\mathbb{M}_{(\mathbb{K}')}=\mathbb{M}\otimes_\mathbb{K}\mathbb{K}'$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{K}'(\mathbb{M})$. ([§Algebraic Extensions, ⁋Proposition 10](/en/math/field_theory/algebraic_extensions#prop10){: data-lid="uqlr5" }) On the other hand, since $\mathbb{K}'(\mathbb{M})/\mathbb{K}'$ has finite degree, it is étale from the assumption that $\mathbb{L}'/\mathbb{K}'$ is separable. Now since étale morphisms are stable under base change, we obtain the desired result. ([§Étale Algebras, ⁋Corollary 14](/en/math/field_theory/etale_algebras#cor14){: data-lid="f1b40" })
:::
