## Graded Rings

If the index set $I$ is a commutative monoid, we agreed to call a family of abelian groups $(A_i)_{i\in I}$ a *graded abelian group*. ([§Abelian Groups, ⁋Definition 16](/en/math/algebraic_structures/abelian_groups#def16){: data-relation="weak" }) At the time, since there were no conditions on $A_i$, this was not a particularly interesting definition; but now that a multiplication structure is added on $A_i$, this definition becomes more meaningful. 

::: Definition 1
Let a commutative monoid $I$ and an $I$-indexed family of abelian groups $(A_i)_{i\in I}$ be given. If a multiplication structure defined on $A=\bigoplus_{i\in I} A_i$ makes it into a ring, and additionally satisfies the condition

$$A_i A_j\subseteq A_{i+j}\qquad\text{for all $i,j\in I$}$$

then we call $A$ an $I$-indexed *graded ring*. Elements belonging to $A_i$ are called *homogeneous elements*.
:::

By definition, any element of $A$ can be uniquely expressed as a finite sum of homogeneous elements. 

::: Proposition 2
If every element of $I$ is cancellable and $A=\bigoplus_{i\in I} A_i$ is a graded ring, then $A_0$ is a subring of $A$. 
:::
::: Proof
From $A_0A_0\subseteq A_0$, $A_0$ is closed under multiplication. Thus it suffices to show that in $A=\bigoplus A_i$, the multiplicative identity $1$ belongs to $A_0$. Let $1=\sum_{i\in I} \epsilon_i$. Then for any $\alpha\in A_j$, 

$$\alpha=1\alpha=\sum_{i\in I} \epsilon_i\alpha\in A_j$$

and therefore for all $i\neq 0$, $\epsilon_i\alpha=0$, while only for $i=0$, $\epsilon_0\alpha=\alpha$ holds. Now since any element of $A$ can be expressed as a sum of homogeneous elements, for all $x\in A$ we have $\epsilon_0x=x$, and substituting $x=1$ yields $1=\epsilon_0\in A_0$. 
:::

In most cases, what we are interested in is the case where $I=\mathbb{Z}$ or $I= \mathbb{N}$. Therefore, the hypothesis of [Proposition 2](#prop2){: data-relation="required" } is satisfied. 

::: Example 3
For any abelian group $G$, consider the free ring generated by $G$, $F(G)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0} G^{\otimes n}$. Since the product of an element of $G^{\otimes m}$ and an element of $G^{\otimes n}$ belongs to $G^{\otimes (m+n)}$, $F(G)$ is an $\mathbb{N}$-graded ring. ([§Definition of a Ring, §§Free Rings over Abelian Groups](/en/math/algebraic_structures/rings#free-rings-over-abelian-groups){: data-relation="weak" }) 
:::

## Graded Ring Homomorphisms

::: Definition 4
For a commutative monoid $I$ and two $I$-graded rings $A,A'$, a ring homomorphism $\phi:A \rightarrow A'$ is a *graded homomorphism* if for every $i\in I$, $\phi(A_i)\subseteq A_i'$ holds.
:::

It is not difficult to see that $I$-graded rings and $I$-graded homomorphisms form a category $\bgr_I\Ring$. 

## Homogeneous Ideals and Quotients of Graded Rings

For a graded ring $A=\bigoplus_{i\in I} A_i$ and a two-sided ideal of $A$, $\mathfrak{a}$, the quotient ring $A/\mathfrak{a}$ may fail to inherit the grading of $A$. That is, there may not exist a grading on $A/\mathfrak{a}$ that makes the quotient map a graded homomorphism. 

::: Example 5
Fix a commutative ring $A$, and consider the *polynomial ring* whose coefficients are elements of $A$,

$$A[\x]=\\{\alpha_n\x^n+\cdots+\alpha_1\x+\alpha_0\mid n\in\mathbb{N}, \alpha_i\in A\\}$$

Then this carries the structure of a graded ring via the decomposition

$$A[\x]=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0} A\x^n$$

On the other hand, consider the ideal generated by $\x-1$, $(\x-1)$. Then as rings

$$A[\x]/(\x-1)\cong A$$

and explicitly, this isomorphism is obtained from the evaluation map defined by

$$\alpha_n\x^n +\cdots+\alpha_1\x+\alpha_0\quad \mapsto\quad \alpha_n+\cdots+\alpha_1+\alpha_0$$

by applying the first isomorphism theorem. However, in this case, the quotient map $\pi:A[\x]\rightarrow A[\x]/(\x-1)$ sends, for any $n$, $A\x^n$ to all of $A$. Therefore, if $A\neq 0$, the sum of the $\pi(A\x^n)$ cannot be a direct sum, and a grading that makes $\pi$ a graded homomorphism does not exist on $A[\x]/(\x-1)$.
:::

To avoid this, we introduce the notion of a *homogeneous ideal*.

::: Proposition 6
For an $I$-graded ring $A=\bigoplus_{i\in I} A_i$ and a two-sided ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ of $A$, the following are all equivalent.

1. $\mathfrak{a}$ is the sum of the $\mathfrak{a}\cap A_i$.
2. Whenever an arbitrary element of $\mathfrak{a}$ is decomposed into homogeneous elements, each of these elements also belongs to $\mathfrak{a}$.
3. $\mathfrak{a}$ is generated by homogeneous elements.
:::
::: Proof
As elements of $A$, all elements of $\mathfrak{a}$ can be uniquely written as a sum of homogeneous elements. Therefore, the first two conditions are equivalent to each other, and under condition 1, $\mathfrak{a}$ is generated by the elements of $\mathfrak{a}\cap A_i$, which are homogeneous elements, so condition 1 implies condition 3. Now we assume the third condition and prove the second. Suppose that $\mathfrak{a}$ is generated by homogeneous elements $(x_j)_{j\in J}$. Then an arbitrary $x\in \mathfrak{a}$ is written in the form

$$x=\sum_{k\in K} a_k x_{j(k)} b_k,\qquad\text{$K$ finite, $a_k,b_k\in A$}$$

Now each of $a_k$ and $b_k$, again as elements of $A$, is written as a sum of homogeneous elements

$$a_k=\sum_{p} a_{kp},\qquad b_k=\sum_q b_{kq}$$

so we obtain

$$x=\sum_{k\in K}\sum_{p,q}a_{kp}x_{j(k)}b_{kq}$$

Here, each $a_{kp}x_{j(k)}b_{kq}$ is a homogeneous element and all belong to $\mathfrak{a}$. Now, grouping together terms of the same degree gives each homogeneous component of $x$, and since these all belong to $\mathfrak{a}$, we obtain condition 2.
:::

A two-sided ideal satisfying the above equivalent conditions is called a *homogeneous ideal*. Then the following holds.

::: Proposition 7
For a homogeneous ideal $\mathfrak{a}$, $A/\mathfrak{a}$ is a graded ring, and its decomposition is given by the formula

$$A/\mathfrak{a}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}A_i/(\mathfrak{a}\cap A_i)$$
:::
::: Proof
Let $\pi:A\rightarrow A/\mathfrak{a}$ be the quotient map. Since $A=\bigoplus_i A_i$ and $\pi$ is surjective, $A/\mathfrak{a}$ is the sum of the $\pi(A_i)$, and because the kernel of the restriction of $\pi$ to $A_i$ is $\mathfrak{a}\cap A_i$, we have $\pi(A_i)\cong A_i/(\mathfrak{a}\cap A_i)$.

To show that this sum is a direct sum, we assume that a finitely supported family of homogeneous elements $(\alpha_i)$ satisfies $\sum_i \pi(\alpha_i)=0$ and must show that $\pi(\alpha_i)=0$ for all $i$. By assumption, $\sum_i\alpha_i\in\mathfrak{a}$, so by the second condition of [Proposition 6](#prop6){: data-relation="required" } each $\alpha_i$ must belong to $\mathfrak{a}$, and hence to $\mathfrak{a}\cap A_i$. That is, $\pi(\alpha_i)=0$ for all $i$.

Finally, from $A_iA_j\subseteq A_{i+j}$ we obtain $\pi(A_i)\pi(A_j)\subseteq \pi(A_{i+j})$, so this decomposition gives a graded ring structure on $A/\mathfrak{a}$.
:::
