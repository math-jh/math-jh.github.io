We now examine a few additional properties of completion.

## Completion and Cauchy Sequences

::: Theorem 1
Fix a Noetherian ring $A$ and an ideal $\mathfrak{a}$, and let $\widehat{A}$ be the completion of $A$ with respect to $\mathfrak{a}$. Then the following hold.

1. $\widehat{A}$ is Noetherian.
2. $\widehat{A}/\mathfrak{a}^i\widehat{A}=A/\mathfrak{a}^i$ holds for all $i$.
:::
::: Proof
First, since $A$ is Noetherian, $A/\mathfrak{a}$ is also Noetherian and $\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{a}^2$ is a finitely generated $A/\mathfrak{a}$-module. Hence $\gr_\mathfrak{a}A$, as an $A/\mathfrak{a}$-algebra, is generated by $\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{a}^2$, and therefore by [§Basic Notions, ⁋Theorem 12](/en/math/commutative_algebra/basic_notions#thm12), $\gr_\mathfrak{a}A$ is Noetherian. Now, following the notation of [§Completion, §§Definition of Completion](/en/math/commutative_algebra/completion#definition-of-completion), write $\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}_i$ for the kernel of the canonical morphism $\rho_i:\widehat{A} \rightarrow A/\mathfrak{a}^i$, and let $\widehat{\mathcal{J}}$ be the filtration of $\widehat{A}$ formed by these. Then by the isomorphism $\gr_\mathfrak{a}A\cong \gr_{\widehat{\mathcal{J}}}\widehat{A}$ obtained in the same place, we know that $\gr_{\widehat{\mathcal{J}}}\widehat{A}$ is also Noetherian. Meanwhile, for any ideal $\mathfrak{b}\subseteq \widehat{A}$, the initial ideal $\initial(\mathfrak{b})$ is generated by finitely many elements by the above argument, so the first result follows from [§Completion, ⁋Proposition 7](/en/math/commutative_algebra/completion#prop7).

For the second result, since $A$ is Noetherian, we can choose finitely many elements such that $\mathfrak{a}^i=(a_1,\ldots, a_n)$. First, since $\rho_i$ sends $\mathfrak{a}^i\widehat{A}$ to $0$, we have $\mathfrak{a}^i\widehat{A}\subseteq \widehat{\mathfrak{a}}_i$. Meanwhile, an element of $\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}_i$ has initial form of degree at least $i$, and conversely a homogeneous element of degree $d\geq i$ belongs, via $\mathfrak{a}^d=\mathfrak{a}^{d-i}\mathfrak{a}^i$, to the ideal generated by $\initial(a_1),\ldots, \initial(a_n)$; hence inside $\gr_{\widehat{\mathcal{J}}}\widehat{A}=\gr_\mathfrak{a}A$, $\initial(\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}_i)$ is generated by $\initial(a_1),\ldots, \initial(a_n)$. Therefore, by [§Completion, ⁋Proposition 7](/en/math/commutative_algebra/completion#prop7), $\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}_i$ is generated by $a_1,\ldots, a_n$, that is, $\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}_i=\mathfrak{a}^i\widehat{A}$. Now since $\widehat{A}/\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}_i\cong A/\mathfrak{a}^i$, we obtain the desired result.
:::

The following lemma can be proved easily by using the topological structure and base of completion discussed in [§Completion, §§The $\mathfrak{a}$-adic Topology](/en/math/commutative_algebra/completion#the-mathfraka-adic-topology), together with [\[Topology\] §Bases of Topological Spaces, ⁋Proposition 2](/en/math/topology/topological_bases#prop2), to check when two bases of a topological space define the same topology.

::: Lemma 2
Suppose we are given two filtrations of an $A$-module $M$ by submodules,

$$\mathcal{J}:\qquad M=M_0\supseteq M_1\supseteq M_2\supseteq\cdots$$

and

$$\mathcal{J}': \qquad M=M_0'\supseteq M_1'\supseteq M_2'\supseteq\cdots$$

If for each $M_i$ there exists some $M_j'$ with $M_j'\subseteq M_i$, and for each $M_i'$ there exists some $M_j$ with $M_j\subseteq M_i'$, then $\widehat{M}_\mathcal{J}\cong \widehat{M}_{\mathcal{J}'}$ holds.
:::

Meanwhile, by [\[Category Theory\] §Limits, ⁋Proposition 10](/en/math/category_theory/limits#prop10), taking completion is left exact. The following lemma shows that, given an appropriate kind of finiteness assumption, taking completion is also right exact.

::: Lemma 3
Fix a Noetherian ring $A$ and an ideal $\mathfrak{a}$. Then for a short exact sequence of finitely generated $A$-modules

$$0 \rightarrow M' \xrightarrow{\alpha} M \rightarrow M'' \rightarrow 0$$

the following sequence

$$0 \rightarrow \varprojlim M'/\mathfrak{a}^i M' \rightarrow \varprojlim M/\mathfrak{a}^i M \rightarrow \varprojlim M''/\mathfrak{a}^i M'' \rightarrow 0$$

is an exact sequence.
:::
::: Proof
Since $\alpha$ is injective, regarding $M'$ as a submodule of $M$, and since $\mathfrak{a}^iM''$ is the image of $\mathfrak{a}^iM$, we obtain for each $i$ the following short exact sequence

$$0 \rightarrow M'/(M'\cap \mathfrak{a}^iM) \rightarrow M/\mathfrak{a}^iM \rightarrow M''/\mathfrak{a}^iM'' \rightarrow 0$$

Applying the left exactness of completion discussed above, we know that $\varprojlim M'/(M'\cap \mathfrak{a}^iM) \rightarrow \varprojlim M/\mathfrak{a}^iM$ is injective and that its image equals the kernel of $\varprojlim M/\mathfrak{a}^iM \rightarrow \varprojlim M''/\mathfrak{a}^iM''$. Meanwhile, since $\{\mathfrak{a}^iM\}$ is an $\mathfrak{a}$-stable filtration, by [§Blowup Algebras, ⁋Lemma 7](/en/math/commutative_algebra/blowup_algebra#lem7), $\{M'\cap \mathfrak{a}^iM\}$ is also $\mathfrak{a}$-stable, and therefore there exists some $l$ such that $M'\cap \mathfrak{a}^iM=\mathfrak{a}^{i-l}(M'\cap \mathfrak{a}^lM)$ holds for all $i\geq l$. From this we obtain

$$\mathfrak{a}^iM'\subseteq M'\cap \mathfrak{a}^iM,\qquad M'\cap \mathfrak{a}^{i+l}M\subseteq \mathfrak{a}^iM'$$

so the two filtrations $\{\mathfrak{a}^iM'\}$ and $\{M'\cap \mathfrak{a}^iM\}$ satisfy the condition of [Lemma 2](#lem2), and the resulting isomorphism $\varprojlim M'/\mathfrak{a}^iM'\cong \varprojlim M'/(M'\cap \mathfrak{a}^iM)$ is compatible with the maps into $\varprojlim M/\mathfrak{a}^iM$ induced by $M'\subseteq M$. Therefore it suffices to show that $\varprojlim M/\mathfrak{a}^i M \rightarrow \varprojlim M''/\mathfrak{a}^i M''$ is surjective.

Suppose an element $(c_i+\mathfrak{a}^i M'')$ of $\varprojlim M''/\mathfrak{a}^i M''$ is given, and let us find $(b_i+\mathfrak{a}^iM)$ satisfying the following two conditions:

- $b_i\mapsto c_i\pmod{\mathfrak{a}^i M''}$,
- $b_i\equiv b_j\pmod{\mathfrak{a}^iM}$ for $i<j$

It is clear that for a fixed $i$ we can find $b_i$ satisfying only the first condition, and that for the second condition it suffices to check the case $j=i+1$. Therefore, suppose inductively that we have found $b_1,\ldots, b_k$ satisfying these two conditions, and let us find $b_{k+1}$. First, choose $b_{k+1}'$ satisfying $b_{k+1}'\mapsto c_{k+1}\pmod{\mathfrak{a}^{k+1} M''}$. Then $b_{k+1}'$ and $b_k$ map to the same element in $M''/\mathfrak{a}^k M''$, so from the exact sequence

$$M'/\mathfrak{a}^{k}M'  \rightarrow M/ \mathfrak{a}^{k}M \rightarrow M''/ \mathfrak{a}^{k}M'' \rightarrow 0$$

we can find some $m'\in M'$ such that $m'$ maps to $b_k-b_{k+1}'$ in $M/\mathfrak{a}^kM$. Now setting $b_{k+1}=b_{k+1}'+\alpha(m')$ gives the desired result.
:::

From this we obtain the following theorem.

::: Theorem 4
Fix a Noetherian ring $A$ and an ideal $\mathfrak{a}$, and let $\widehat{A}$ be the completion of $A$ with respect to $\mathfrak{a}$. Then the following hold.

1. For any finitely generated $A$-module $M$,

    $$\widehat{A}\otimes_A M \rightarrow\varprojlim_i M/\mathfrak{a}^iM$$

    is an isomorphism.
2. $\widehat{A}$ is a flat $A$-module.
:::
::: Proof
Since both $\varprojlim$ and $\otimes$ commute with finite direct sums, the first result holds trivially when $M$ is a finitely generated free module. Now for an arbitrary finitely generated $A$-module $M$, applying $\widehat{A}\otimes_A-$ to a free presentation

$$F \rightarrow G \rightarrow M \rightarrow 0$$

of $M$, we obtain the desired result by [Lemma 3](#lem3) and [\[Homological Algebra\] §Diagram Chasing, ⁋Proposition 1](/en/math/homological_algebra/diagram_chasing#prop1).

For the second result, by [§Flatness, ⁋Proposition 1](/en/math/commutative_algebra/flatness#prop1), it is equivalent to showing that $\widehat{\mathfrak{a}} \rightarrow \widehat{A}$ is injective for every finitely generated ideal $\mathfrak{a}$, and this in turn is immediate from the left exactness of completion discussed in [Lemma 3](#lem3).
:::

## Hensel's Lemma

A typical example of a complete ring is the ring of formal power series $A[[\x_i]]_{i\in I}$ discussed in [§Completion, ⁋Example 4](/en/math/commutative_algebra/completion#ex4). Meanwhile, we saw in [\[Algebraic Structures\] §Algebras, ⁋Proposition 8](/en/math/algebraic_structures/algebras#prop8) that the polynomial ring $A[\x_i]_{i\in I}$ plays the role of a free functor $\Set \rightarrow \cAlg{A}$; a similar kind of universal property holds for $A[[\x_i]]_{i\in I}$ as well.

::: Theorem 5
Fix a ring $A$ and an $A$-algebra $E$, and suppose that $E$ is complete with respect to some ideal $\mathfrak{a}\subseteq E$. Let $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\in \mathfrak{a}$. Then the following hold.

1. There exists a unique $A$-algebra homomorphism $\phi:A[[\x_1,\ldots, \x_n]]\rightarrow E$ sending each $\x_i$ to $\alpha_i$.
2. If $A \rightarrow E/\mathfrak{a}$ is surjective and the $\alpha_i$ generate $\mathfrak{a}$, then $\phi$ is also surjective.
3. If $\gr\phi: A[\x_1,\ldots, \x_n]\cong \gr_{(\x_1,\ldots, \x_n)}A[[\x_1,\ldots, \x_n]] \rightarrow \gr_\mathfrak{a}E$ is a monomorphism, then so is $\phi$.
:::
::: Proof
The first claim is almost identical to [\[Algebraic Structures\] §Algebras, ⁋Proposition 8](/en/math/algebraic_structures/algebras#prop8), and like that proposition it holds for infinitely many variables as well.

For the second claim, from the given assumptions we know that

$$(\x_1,\ldots, \x_n)/(\x_1,\ldots, \x_n)^2 \rightarrow \mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{a}^2$$

is surjective, and since $\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{a}^2$ generates $\gr_\mathfrak{a}E$, we know that $\gr\phi$ is surjective. Now choose an arbitrary $y\in E$. Since the case $y=0$ is trivial, suppose $y\neq 0$; then since $E$ is complete, $E \rightarrow \widehat{E}$ is injective and therefore $\bigcap_i \mathfrak{a}^i=0$, so there exists a largest $i$ such that $y\in \mathfrak{a}^i$. Hence we can find $x_1\in (\x_1,\ldots, \x_n)^i$ such that the initial form of $x_1$ is sent by $\gr\phi$ to the initial form of $y$, and then $y-\phi(x_1)\in \mathfrak{a}^{i+1}$. Repeating this process, we obtain an infinite family $(x_1,x_2,\ldots)$ of elements of $A[\x_1,\ldots, \x_n]$ satisfying the sum

$$y=\sum_{j=1}^\infty \phi(x_j)=\phi\left(\sum_{j=1}^\infty x_j\right)$$

Here, since the degree of $x_j$ increases with $j$, $\sum_j x_j$ defines an element of $A[[\x_1,\ldots, \x_n]]$, and since $\phi$ sends $(\x_1,\ldots, \x_n)^i$ into $\mathfrak{a}^i$, we may interchange $\phi$ with the infinite sum as in the second equality above.

For the last claim, if $x$ is a nonzero element of $A[[\x_1,\ldots, \x_n]]$, then $\initial(x)$ is also nonzero, and therefore $(\gr\phi)(\initial(x))$ is also nonzero. Now, letting $d$ be the degree of $\initial(x)$, since

$$x\equiv \initial(x)\pmod{(\x_1,\ldots, \x_n)^{d+1}}$$

we have

$$\phi(x)\equiv(\gr\phi)(\initial(x))\pmod{\mathfrak{a}^{d+1}}$$

and hence $\phi(x)\neq 0$.
:::

Then the following holds.

::: Corollary 6
Fix a power series $f\in \x A[[\x]]$, and define $\phi: A[[\x]] \rightarrow A[[\x]]$ by the formula

$$\phi: A[[\x]] \rightarrow A[[\x]];\qquad \x\mapsto f$$

Then $\phi$ is an isomorphism if and only if $f'(0)$ is a unit in $A$.
:::
::: Proof
First, the elements of $A[[\x]]$ not belonging to $(\x)$ are exactly those with nonzero constant term, and a $\phi$ of the form given in the condition preserves such elements. Now if $\phi$ is an isomorphism, it follows that $\phi((\x))=(\x)$. Moreover, $\phi$ must send a generator of $(\x)$ again to a generator of $(\x)$, so it follows that $f+(\x^2)$ must generate $(\x)/(\x^2)$, and now since

$$f\equiv f'(0)\x\pmod{\x^2}$$

it follows that $f'(0)$ must be a unit of $A$.

Conversely, suppose $f'(0)$ is a unit of $A$. First, $\gr_{(\x)}A[[\x]]\cong \gr_{(\x)}A[\x]=A[\x]$, and setting $u=f'(0)$, the map $\gr\phi: A[\x] \rightarrow A[\x]$ sends $\x$ to $u\x$ by definition. Now $\phi$ is injective by the third result of [Theorem 5](#thm5), and writing $f=u\x+h\x^2=(u+h\x)\x$ for some $h\in A[[\x]]$, we know that $f$ generates $(\x)$. Therefore we obtain the desired conclusion again by the second result of [Theorem 5](#thm5).
:::

We then obtain the following theorem, the central result of this section.

::: Theorem 7 (Hensel)
Suppose a ring $A$ is complete with respect to an ideal $\mathfrak{a}$, and let $f(\x)\in A[\x]$ and $a\in A$. If

$$f(a)\equiv 0\pmod{f'(a)^2 \mathfrak{a}}$$

then there exists some $b\in A$ such that

$$f(b)=0,\qquad b\equiv a\pmod{f'(a)\mathfrak{a}}$$

holds. Moreover, if $f'(a)$ is a non-zerodivisor, then such a $b$ is uniquely determined.
:::
::: Proof
For convenience of notation, write $f'(a)=e$. Then from

$$f(a+e\x)=f(a)+f'(a)e\x+\cdots$$

we can choose $h$ such that $f(a+e\x)=f(a)+f'(a)e\x+h(\x)(e\x)^2$. Then

$$f(a+e\x)=f(a)+e^2(\x+\x^2h(\x))$$

and by the first result of [Theorem 5](#thm5), there exists a unique $A$-algebra homomorphism $\phi:A[[\x]] \rightarrow A[[\x]]$ sending $\x$ to $\x+\x^2h(\x)$. Meanwhile, by [Corollary 6](#cor6), $\phi$ is an isomorphism, and therefore its inverse $\phi^{-1}$ exists. Now applying $\phi^{-1}$ to the above equation, we obtain

$$f(a+e\phi^{-1}(\x))=f(a)+e^2\x$$

and by the given assumption there exists $\alpha\in \mathfrak{a}$ such that $f(a)=e^2\alpha$. Using the first result of [Theorem 5](#thm5) again, take the $A$-algebra homomorphism $\psi: A[[\x]] \rightarrow A$ sending $\x$ to $-\alpha$; then from the above equation we obtain

$$f(a+e\psi(\phi^{-1}(\x)))=0$$

Therefore, setting $b=a+e\psi(\phi^{-1}(\x))$ gives the desired result. Here, since $\phi^{-1}(\x)\in (\x)$ and $\psi((\x))\subseteq \mathfrak{a}$, we also have $b-a\in e\mathfrak{a}$, that is, $b\equiv a\pmod{f'(a)\mathfrak{a}}$.

For uniqueness, suppose $e$ is not a zero divisor and let $b,b'$ be two elements satisfying the given conditions. Then by definition they must be of the form $a+er$, $a+er'$, respectively. Now applying the first result of [Theorem 5](#thm5), choose $\beta,\beta': A[[\x]] \rightarrow A$ sending $\x$ to $r$ and $r'$, respectively. Applying them, since

$$f(a)+e^2(r+r^2h(r))=f(a+er)=0=f(a+er')=f(a)+e^2(r'+(r')^2h(r'))$$

we obtain $\beta(\phi(\x))=\beta'(\phi(\x))$. Now the desired result follows from the fact that $\phi$ is an isomorphism and the uniqueness in [Theorem 5](#thm5).
:::

We conclude by mentioning the following theorem.

::: Theorem 8 (Cohen structure theorem)
For a complete local Noetherian ring $(A, \mathfrak{m})$ with residue field $\kappa$, if $A$ contains some field, then there exist some $n$ and an ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ such that $A\cong\kappa[[\x_1,\ldots, \x_n]]/\mathfrak{a}$.
:::
