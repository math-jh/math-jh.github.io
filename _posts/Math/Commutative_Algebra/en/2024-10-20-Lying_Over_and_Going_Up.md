## Lying over, going up

::: Proposition 1
Let $A\hookrightarrow B$ be an integral extension.

1. (Lying over) For any prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of $A$, there exists a prime ideal $\mathfrak{q}$ of $B$ such that $\mathfrak{q}\cap A=\mathfrak{p}$.
2. (Going up) The prime ideal $\mathfrak{q}$ above can be chosen so that $\mathfrak{b}\subseteq \mathfrak{q}$ whenever we are given an ideal $\mathfrak{b}$ of $B$ satisfying $\mathfrak{b}\cap A\subseteq \mathfrak{p}$.
:::
::: Proof
We begin with the second statement. If $A\hookrightarrow B$ is an integral extension, then for any ideal $\mathfrak{b}$ of $B$, the induced ring homomorphism

$$\frac{A}{A\cap \mathfrak{b}}\hookrightarrow \frac{B}{\mathfrak{b}}$$ 

is again an integral extension. Hence, without loss of generality, we may assume $\mathfrak{b}=0$, which is precisely the first statement.

Thus, given a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}\subseteq A$, it suffices to find a prime ideal $\mathfrak{q}$ of $B$ satisfying $\mathfrak{q}\cap A=\mathfrak{p}$. 

Now let $S=A\setminus \mathfrak{p}$. If $A \hookrightarrow B$ is integral, then so is $S^{-1}A \rightarrow S^{-1}B$, so it suffices to treat the case where $A$ is a local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{p}$. In this situation, the preimage of any maximal ideal of $B$ containing $\mathfrak{p}B$ must equal $\mathfrak{p}$, so unless $\mathfrak{p}B=B$, such a maximal ideal is the prime ideal of $B$ we seek. 

Suppose, for contradiction, that $\mathfrak{p}B=B$. Then $1\in B$ can be written as a $B$-linear combination of elements of $\mathfrak{p}$:

$$1=\sum_{i=1}^n b_i a_i,\qquad a_i\in \mathfrak{p},\quad b_i\in B$$

Let $B'$ be the $A$-subalgebra of $B$ generated by the $b_i$. Every element of $B'$ is integral over $A$, and $B'$ is finitely generated as an $A$-algebra. Hence, by [§Integral Extensions, ⁋Lemma 4](/en/math/commutative_algebra/integral_extension#lem4), $B'$ is finitely generated as an $A$-module. Applying [§Integral Extensions, ⁋Lemma 8](/en/math/commutative_algebra/integral_extension#lem8) then gives $B'=0$, a contradiction. 
:::

The central result of this post is [Corollary 4](#cor4), which says, roughly, that if two prime ideals $\mathfrak{q}_1, \mathfrak{q}_2$ of $B$ lie over the same prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of $A$ as in [Proposition 1](#prop1), then neither contains the other. 

::: Lemma 2
Let $A\subseteq B$ be integral domains. If $\Frac(A) \rightarrow \Frac(B)$ is an algebraic extension, then every nonzero ideal of $B$ meets $A$ nontrivially.
:::
::: Proof
It suffices to consider principal ideals of $B$. Take the principal ideal generated by an arbitrary $b\in B$. Since $\Frac(B)$ is an algebraic extension of $\Frac(A)$, we may write

$$a_nb^n+\cdots+a_1b+a_0=0,\qquad a_i\in \Frac(A)$$

Multiplying both sides by the product of the denominators of the $a_i$ and, if necessary, dividing both sides by a suitable power of $b$, we may assume that every $a_i$ lies in $A$ and that $a_0\neq 0$. Then $a_0$ belongs to the principal ideal generated by $b$.
:::

::: Corollary 3
Let $A\hookrightarrow B$ be an integral extension. Then a prime ideal $\mathfrak{q}$ of $B$ is maximal if and only if $\mathfrak{q}\cap A$ is a maximal ideal of $A$.
:::
::: Proof
As in the proof of [Proposition 1](#prop1), we pass to the quotients by $\mathfrak{q}\cap A$ and $\mathfrak{q}$, respectively. Since $\mathfrak{q}$ is prime, $B/\mathfrak{q}$ is a domain, and $A/(\mathfrak{q}\cap A)$, being a subring of it, is also a domain. Hence it suffices to show that, given integral domains $A,B$ and an integral extension $A \hookrightarrow B$, $A$ is a field if and only if $B$ is a field. On the one hand, if $A$ is a field, then by [Lemma 2](#lem2), $B$ cannot have any nonzero proper ideal; that is, $B$ is a field. 

So assume that $B$ is a field; we show that $A$ is a field. Let $\mathfrak{m}$ be a maximal ideal of $A$. By [Proposition 1](#prop1), there exists a prime ideal $\mathfrak{q}$ of $B$ such that $\mathfrak{q}\cap A= \mathfrak{m}$. But $B$ is a field, so $\mathfrak{q}=0$, and hence $\mathfrak{m}=0$. This gives the desired result. 
:::

Finally, consider the following. 

::: Corollary 4
Let $A\hookrightarrow B$ be an integral extension. If two prime ideals $\mathfrak{q}_1\neq \mathfrak{q}_2$ of $B$ satisfy $A\cap \mathfrak{q}_1=A\cap \mathfrak{q}_2=\mathfrak{p}$, then $\mathfrak{q}_1\not\subseteq \mathfrak{q}_2$ and $\mathfrak{q}_2\not\subseteq \mathfrak{q}_1$.
:::
::: Proof
Suppose, for contradiction, that $\mathfrak{q}_1\subseteq \mathfrak{q}_2$, and write $A\cap \mathfrak{q}_1=A\cap \mathfrak{q}_2=\mathfrak{p}$. Taking the quotient of $A$ by $\mathfrak{p}$ and of $B$ by $\mathfrak{q}_1$, we may reduce to the case where $B$ is an integral domain, $\mathfrak{q}_1=0$, and $\mathfrak{q}_2\cap A=0$. The integral equations satisfied by elements of $B$ remain integral equations after passing to the quotient by $\mathfrak{p}$; in particular, $\Frac(B)$ is an algebraic extension of $\Frac(A)$. The desired result now follows from [Lemma 2](#lem2).
:::

If [Proposition 1](#prop1) and [Corollary 4](#cor4) concern lifting chains of prime ideals upward, the *going-down* property in the opposite direction fails for general integral extensions. It does hold when the base is an integrally closed domain, i.e., a normal domain ([§Integral Extensions, ⁋Definition 3](/en/math/commutative_algebra/integral_extension#def3)), and its proof rests on the following lemma.

::: Lemma 5
Let $A\hookrightarrow B$ be an integral extension, with $A$ an integrally closed domain and $B$ a domain. For any $b\in B$, let $f_b\in K[\x]$ be its minimal polynomial over $K=\Frac(A)$.

1. Every coefficient of $f_b$ belongs to $A$.
2. If a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of $A$ satisfies $b\in\mathfrak{p}B$, then every coefficient of $f_b$ other than the leading coefficient belongs to $\mathfrak{p}$.
:::
::: Proof
Since $b$ is integral, there exists a monic polynomial $g\in A[\x]$ having $b$ as a root. In $K[\x]$ we have $f_b\mid g$, and the roots of $f_b$ are all roots of $g$, hence integral over $A$. The coefficients of $f_b$, being symmetric polynomials in these roots, are therefore integral over $A$ as well, and since $A$ is integrally closed, they belong to $A$.

Now suppose $b\in\mathfrak{p}B$. Write $b=\sum_i p_i b_i$ ($p_i\in\mathfrak{p}, b_i\in B$), and let $B'$ be the $A$-subalgebra of $B$ generated by the $b_i$; then $B'$ is a finitely generated $A$-module. Since $b\in\mathfrak{p}B'$, [§Integral Extensions, ⁋Theorem 1](/en/math/commutative_algebra/integral_extension#thm1) yields a monic polynomial $g\in A[\x]$ having $b$ as a root whose non-leading coefficients all lie in $\mathfrak{p}$. By what we showed above, $h=g/f_b\in A[\x]$, and reducing $g=f_b h$ modulo $\mathfrak{p}$ gives $\x^{\deg g}=\overline{f_b}\overline{h}$ in $(A/\mathfrak{p})[\x]$. Since $A/\mathfrak{p}$ is a domain, $\overline{f_b}$ must be a power of $\x$, which means that every coefficient of $f_b$ other than the leading coefficient belongs to $\mathfrak{p}$.
:::

::: Theorem 6 (Going down)
Let $A\hookrightarrow B$ be an integral extension, with $A$ an integrally closed domain and $B$ a domain. Suppose we are given prime ideals $\mathfrak{p}_1\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_2$ of $A$ and a prime ideal $\mathfrak{q}_2$ of $B$ satisfying $\mathfrak{q}_2\cap A=\mathfrak{p}_2$. Then there exists a prime ideal $\mathfrak{q}_1$ of $B$ such that $\mathfrak{q}_1\subseteq\mathfrak{q}_2$ and $\mathfrak{q}_1\cap A=\mathfrak{p}_1$.
:::
::: Proof
Since $B$ is a domain, $B\hookrightarrow B_{\mathfrak{q}_2}$, and composing this with $A\hookrightarrow B$ gives the ring extension $A\hookrightarrow B_{\mathfrak{q}_2}$. By the same argument as in the proof of [Proposition 1](#prop1), once we show that $\mathfrak{p}_1 B_{\mathfrak{q}_2}\cap A\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_1$, there exists a prime ideal of $B_{\mathfrak{q}_2}$ lying over $\mathfrak{p}_1$. Such an ideal corresponds to a prime ideal $\mathfrak{q}_1$ of $B$ contained in $\mathfrak{q}_2$, which gives the desired result.

Let $a\in\mathfrak{p}_1 B_{\mathfrak{q}_2}\cap A$. Write $a=p/s$ with $p\in\mathfrak{p}_1 B$ and $s\in B\setminus\mathfrak{q}_2$, and assume $a\neq 0$. By [Lemma 5](#lem5), the minimal polynomial $f=\x^n+c_{n-1}\x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_0$ of $p$ satisfies $c_i\in\mathfrak{p}_1$ for all $i$. On the other hand, since $a\in A\subseteq K$ and $p=as$, the polynomial obtained from $f$ by the substitution $\x\mapsto a\x$ followed by normalization,

$$\frac{1}{a^n}f(a\x)=\x^n+\frac{c_{n-1}}{a}\x^{n-1}+\cdots+\frac{c_0}{a^n}$$

is irreducible over $K$ and has $s$ as a root. In other words, it is the minimal polynomial of $s$, so by [Lemma 5](#lem5) its coefficients belong to $A$. Writing it as $\x^n+c'_{n-1}\x^{n-1}+\cdots+c'_0$, we have $c'_{n-i}a^i=c_{n-i}\in\mathfrak{p}_1$. If $a\notin\mathfrak{p}_1$, then, since $\mathfrak{p}_1$ is prime, every $c'_{n-i}$ lies in $\mathfrak{p}_1$, and hence $s^n=-c'_{n-1}s^{n-1}-\cdots-c'_0\in\mathfrak{p}_1 B\subseteq\mathfrak{q}_2$, contradicting the fact that $\mathfrak{q}_2$ is prime. Therefore $a\in\mathfrak{p}_1$.
:::
