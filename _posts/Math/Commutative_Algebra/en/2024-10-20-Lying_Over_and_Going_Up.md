## Lying over, going up

::: Proposition 1
Let $A\hookrightarrow B$ be an integral extension.

1. (Lying over) For any prime ideal of $A$, $\mathfrak{p}$, there exists a prime ideal of $B$, $\mathfrak{q}$, such that $\mathfrak{q}\cap A=\mathfrak{p}$.
2. (Going up) The prime ideal $\mathfrak{q}$ obtained in the above proposition can be chosen so that whenever $\mathfrak{b}\cap A\subseteq \mathfrak{p}$ holds for an ideal of $B$, $\mathfrak{b}$, we have $\mathfrak{b}\subseteq \mathfrak{q}$.
:::
::: Proof
First, in the second result, we know that if $A\hookrightarrow B$ is an integral extension, then for any ideal of $B$, $\mathfrak{b}$, the following ring homomorphism

$$\frac{A}{A\cap \mathfrak{b}}\hookrightarrow \frac{B}{\mathfrak{b}}$$ 

is also an integral extension. Hence, without loss of generality, it suffices to assume $\mathfrak{b}=0$, which is precisely showing the first result.

Thus, given a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}\subseteq A$, it suffices to find, satisfying $\mathfrak{q}\cap A=\mathfrak{p}$, a prime ideal of $B$, $\mathfrak{q}$.

Meanwhile, if we let $S=A\setminus \mathfrak{p}$, then if $A \hookrightarrow B$ is integral, so is $S^{-1}A \rightarrow S^{-1}B$. Thus it suffices to consider only the case where $A$ is a local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{p}$. In this situation, since the preimage of any maximal ideal containing $\mathfrak{p}B$ in $B$ must be $\mathfrak{p}$, unless $\mathfrak{p}B=B$, this maximal ideal is the prime ideal of $B$ we seek.

Suppose, for contradiction, that $\mathfrak{p}B=B$. Then $1\in B$ can be written as a linear combination of elements of $\mathfrak{p}$ with coefficients in $B$:

$$1=\sum_{i=1}^n b_i a_i,\qquad a_i\in \mathfrak{p},\quad b_i\in B$$

Now, let the subalgebra generated by the $b_i$ in $B$ over $A$ be $B'$. Then every element of $B'$ is integral, and $B'$ is finitely generated as an $A$-algebra. Hence, by [§Integral Extensions, ⁋Lemma 4](/en/math/commutative_algebra/integral_extension#lem4){: data-lid="j0u0t" }, $B'$ is finitely generated as an $A$-module. Applying [§Integral Extensions, ⁋Lemma 8](/en/math/commutative_algebra/integral_extension#lem8){: data-lid="7t76d" } now gives $B'=0$, a contradiction.
:::

An important part of this post is proving [Corollary 4](#cor4){: data-lid="ushol" }, which roughly states that if, over a prime ideal of $A$, $\mathfrak{p}$, two prime ideals of $B$, $\mathfrak{q}_1, \mathfrak{q}_2$, obtained through [Proposition 1](#prop1){: data-lid="nuzf6" } are given, they do not contain each other.

::: Lemma 2
For two integral domains $A\subseteq B$, if $\Frac(A) \rightarrow \Frac(B)$ is an algebraic extension, then every nonzero ideal of $B$ meets $A$ nontrivially.
:::
::: Proof
It suffices to consider only principal ideals of $B$. Consider any principal ideal generated by $b\in B$. Then, since $\Frac(B)$ is an algebraic extension of $\Frac(A)$, we can arrange that

$$a_nb^n+\cdots+a_1b+a_0=0,\qquad a_i\in \Frac(A)$$

holds. Now multiplying both sides by the product of the denominators of the $a_i$ and, if necessary, dividing both sides appropriately by a power of $b$, we can arrange that each $a_i$ is an element of $A$ and $a_0\neq 0$. Then $a_0$ belongs to the principal ideal generated by $b$.
:::

::: Corollary 3
Let $A\hookrightarrow B$ be an integral extension. Then in $B$, a prime ideal $\mathfrak{q}$ is a maximal ideal if and only if $\mathfrak{q}\cap A$ is a maximal ideal of $A$.
:::
::: Proof
As in the proof of [Proposition 1](#prop1){: data-lid="i9e5l" }, here too we pass to the quotients by $\mathfrak{q}\cap A$ and $\mathfrak{q}$, respectively. Since $\mathfrak{q}$ is prime, $B/\mathfrak{q}$ is a domain, and $A/(\mathfrak{q}\cap A)$, being its subring, is also a domain. Hence it suffices to show that, given two integral domains $A,B$ and an integral extension $A \hookrightarrow B$, $A$ is a field if and only if $B$ is a field. On the other hand, if $A$ is a field, then by [Lemma 2](#lem2){: data-lid="rlay2" }, $B$ cannot have any nonzero proper ideal; that is, $B$ is a field. 

Therefore, it suffices to assume that $B$ is a field and show that $A$ is a field. In $A$, let a maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ be given. Then by [Proposition 1](#prop1){: data-lid="30gn7" }, we can find in $B$ a prime ideal $\mathfrak{q}$ such that $\mathfrak{q}\cap A= \mathfrak{m}$. But since $B$ is a field, $\mathfrak{q}=0$, and hence $\mathfrak{m}=0$. From this we obtain the desired result. 
:::

Finally, consider the following. 

::: Corollary 4
For an integral extension $A\hookrightarrow B$, if in $B$ two prime ideals $\mathfrak{q}_1\neq \mathfrak{q}_2$ satisfy $A\cap \mathfrak{q}_1=A\cap \mathfrak{q}_2=\mathfrak{p}$, then $\mathfrak{q}_1\not\subseteq \mathfrak{q}_2$ and $\mathfrak{q}_2\not\subseteq \mathfrak{q}_1$.
:::
::: Proof
Suppose, contrary to the conclusion, that $\mathfrak{q}_1\subseteq \mathfrak{q}_2$, and let $A\cap \mathfrak{q}_1=A\cap \mathfrak{q}_2=\mathfrak{p}$. Then, taking the quotient of $A$ by $\mathfrak{p}$ and of $B$ by $\mathfrak{q}_1$, we can reduce the situation to the case where $B$ is an integral domain, $\mathfrak{q}_1=0$, and $\mathfrak{q}_2\cap A=0$. However, the integral equations satisfied by elements of $B$ remain integral equations even after passing to the quotient by $\mathfrak{p}$; in particular, $\Frac(B)$ is an algebraic extension of $\Frac(A)$. Therefore, by [Lemma 2](#lem2){: data-lid="me31z" }, we obtain the desired result.
:::

While [Proposition 1](#prop1){: data-lid="6d8fj" } and [Corollary 4](#cor4){: data-lid="qo7kf" } are in the direction of lifting chains of prime ideals upward, *going-down* in the opposite direction does not hold for general integral extensions. It does hold, however, when the base is an integrally closed domain, that is, a normal domain ([§Integral Extensions, ⁋Definition 3](/en/math/commutative_algebra/integral_extension#def3){: data-lid="e8o7l" }), and its proof rests on the following lemma.

::: Lemma 5
Let $A\hookrightarrow B$ be an integral extension, where $A$ is an integrally closed domain and $B$ is also a domain. For any $b\in B$, let its minimal polynomial over $K=\Frac(A)$ be $f_b\in K[\x]$.

1. Every coefficient of $f_b$ belongs to $A$.
2. If $b\in\mathfrak{p}B$ for a prime ideal of $A$, $\mathfrak{p}$, then all coefficients of $f_b$ other than the leading coefficient belong to $\mathfrak{p}$.
:::
::: Proof
Since $b$ is integral, there exists, with $b$ as a root, a monic polynomial $g\in A[\x]$. In $K[\x]$, we have $f_b\mid g$, and the roots of $f_b$ are all roots of $g$, hence integral over $A$. Therefore, the coefficients of $f_b$, being symmetric polynomials in these roots, are also integral over $A$, and since $A$ is integrally closed, they belong to $A$.

Now suppose $b\in\mathfrak{p}B$. Write $b=\sum_i p_i b_i$ ($p_i\in\mathfrak{p}, b_i\in B$), and if we let the subalgebra generated by the $b_i$ in $B$ over $A$ be $B'$, then $B'$ is a finitely generated $A$-module. Since $b\in\mathfrak{p}B'$, by [§Integral Extensions, ⁋Theorem 1](/en/math/commutative_algebra/integral_extension#thm1){: data-lid="yzem3" }, we obtain, having $b$ as a root and all coefficients other than the leading coefficient belonging to $\mathfrak{p}$, a monic polynomial $g\in A[\x]$. By what was shown above, $h=g/f_b\in A[\x]$, and reducing $g=f_b h$ modulo $\mathfrak{p}$ yields, in $(A/\mathfrak{p})[\x]$, $\x^{\deg g}=\overline{f_b}\overline{h}$. Since $A/\mathfrak{p}$ is a domain, $\overline{f_b}$ must be a power of $\x$, which means that all coefficients of $f_b$ other than the leading coefficient belong to $\mathfrak{p}$.
:::

::: Theorem 6 (Going down)
Let $A\hookrightarrow B$ be an integral extension, where $A$ is an integrally closed domain and $B$ is a domain. Suppose we are given prime ideals of $A$, $\mathfrak{p}_1\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_2$, and, satisfying $\mathfrak{q}_2\cap A=\mathfrak{p}_2$, a prime ideal of $B$, $\mathfrak{q}_2$. Then there exists, satisfying $\mathfrak{q}_1\subseteq\mathfrak{q}_2$ and $\mathfrak{q}_1\cap A=\mathfrak{p}_1$, a prime ideal of $B$, $\mathfrak{q}_1$.
:::
::: Proof
Since $B$ is a domain, $B\hookrightarrow B_{\mathfrak{q}_2}$, and composing this with $A\hookrightarrow B$ gives the ring extension $A\hookrightarrow B_{\mathfrak{q}_2}$. By the same argument as in the proof of [Proposition 1](#prop1){: data-lid="htx8t" }, if we show that $\mathfrak{p}_1 B_{\mathfrak{q}_2}\cap A\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_1$, there exists, lying over $\mathfrak{p}_1$, a prime ideal of $B_{\mathfrak{q}_2}$. This corresponds to a prime ideal of $B$ contained in $\mathfrak{q}_2$, $\mathfrak{q}_1$, which gives the desired result.

Let $a\in\mathfrak{p}_1 B_{\mathfrak{q}_2}\cap A$. Write $a=p/s$ ($p\in\mathfrak{p}_1 B$, $s\in B\setminus\mathfrak{q}_2$) and assume $a\neq 0$. By [Lemma 5](#lem5){: data-lid="kdmfy" }, the minimal polynomial of $p$, $f=\x^n+c_{n-1}\x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_0$, satisfies $c_i\in\mathfrak{p}_1$ for all $i$. On the other hand, since $a\in A\subseteq K$ and $p=as$, the polynomial obtained from $f$ by the substitution $\x\mapsto a\x$ followed by normalization,

$$\frac{1}{a^n}f(a\x)=\x^n+\frac{c_{n-1}}{a}\x^{n-1}+\cdots+\frac{c_0}{a^n}$$

is irreducible over $K$ and has $s$ as a root. In other words, this is the minimal polynomial of $s$, and by [Lemma 5](#lem5){: data-lid="x6cru" } its coefficients belong to $A$. Writing this as $\x^n+c'_{n-1}\x^{n-1}+\cdots+c'_0$, we have $c'_{n-i}a^i=c_{n-i}\in\mathfrak{p}_1$. If $a\notin\mathfrak{p}_1$, then since $\mathfrak{p}_1$ is prime, all $c'_{n-i}\in\mathfrak{p}_1$, and thus $s^n=-c'_{n-1}s^{n-1}-\cdots-c'_0\in\mathfrak{p}_1 B\subseteq\mathfrak{q}_2$, contradicting the fact that $\mathfrak{q}_2$ is prime. Therefore $a\in\mathfrak{p}_1$.
:::
