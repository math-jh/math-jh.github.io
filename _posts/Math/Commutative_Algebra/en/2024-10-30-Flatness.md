We defined the $\Tor$ functors in [\[Homological Algebra\] §Ext and Tor, ⁋Definition 2](/en/math/homological_algebra/ext_and_tor#def2){: data-lid="hlc93" }, and we know that if $B$ is a flat $A$-algebra, the following canonical isomorphism

$$B\otimes_A\Tor_i^A(M,N)\cong\Tor_i^B(B\otimes_AM, B\otimes_AN)$$

exists. This applies particularly well to the case $B=S^{-1}A$.

::: Proposition 1
Let an $A$-module $M$ and an $A$-ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ be given. Then the multiplication map $\mathfrak{a}\otimes_AM \rightarrow M$ is an injection if and only if $\Tor_1^A(A/\mathfrak{a}, M)=0$. $M$ is flat if and only if for every finitely generated $\mathfrak{a}$, $\mathfrak{a}\otimes_AM \rightarrow M$ is an injection.
:::
::: Proof
Taking the short exact sequence

$$0 \rightarrow \mathfrak{a} \rightarrow A \rightarrow A/\mathfrak{a} \rightarrow 0$$

and applying the $\Tor$ long exact sequence, we obtain

$$\cdots \rightarrow \Tor_1^A(A, M) \rightarrow \Tor_1^A(A/\mathfrak{a}, M) \rightarrow \mathfrak{a}\otimes_AM \rightarrow A\otimes_AM \rightarrow (A/\mathfrak{a})\otimes_AM \rightarrow 0$$

From this, it is immediate that $\mathfrak{a}\otimes M \rightarrow M$ being an injection is equivalent to $\Tor_1^A(A/\mathfrak{a},M)=0$.

We now must show the second claim. For this, we must show that for any injection $L \rightarrow N$, $L\otimes_AM \rightarrow N\otimes_AM$ is an injection. However, to show this, it suffices to assume that $L$ and $N$ are both *finitely generated*. Viewing $L$ as a submodule of $N$, suppose $z\in L\otimes_AM$ belongs to the kernel of $L\otimes_AM \rightarrow N\otimes_AM$. Then we can write $z$ as a finite sum $z=\sum_k u_k\otimes m_k$, and taking the finitely generated submodule generated by the $u_k$, $L'\subseteq L$, and an element $z'=\sum_k u_k\otimes m_k\in L'\otimes_AM$, the image of $z'$ in $L\otimes_AM$ is $z$. Meanwhile, $N$ is the direct limit of the finitely generated submodules containing $L'$, denoted $N'$, and since $\otimes$ commutes with colimits by [\[Algebraic Structures\] §Direct Products, Direct Sums, and Tensor Products of Modules, ⁋Theorem 6](/en/math/algebraic_structures/operations_of_modules#thm6){: data-lid="bws5u" }, $N\otimes_AM$ is the direct limit of the $N'\otimes_AM$. Hence, from the fact that the image of $z'$ in $N\otimes_AM$ is $0$, we know that in some suitable $N'$, the image of $z'$ is already $0$. Now if the claim holds for the finitely generated $L'\subseteq N'$, then $z'=0$, and therefore $z=0$.

Now, between two finitely generated $A$-modules $N$, $L$, we can choose a sequence of submodules

$$L=N_0 \subseteq N_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq N_p=N$$

so that each $N_{i+1}/N_i$ is generated by a single element. Then there exists a suitable $A$-ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ such that $N_{i+1}/N_i\cong A/\mathfrak{a}$. Moreover, repeatedly composing these inclusions yields $L\hookrightarrow N$, which is also an injection, so consequently it suffices to assume $p=1$ and $N/L\cong A/\mathfrak{a}$. Also, recall that if for every finitely generated ideal $\mathfrak{a}'$, $\mathfrak{a}'\otimes_AM \rightarrow M$ is an injection, then for every ideal $\mathfrak{a}$, $\mathfrak{a}\otimes_AM \rightarrow M$ is an injection. Indeed, if $z=\sum_k a_k\otimes m_k\in \mathfrak{a}\otimes_AM$ is sent to $0$ by the multiplication map, then for $\mathfrak{a}'=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$, the element $z'=\sum_k a_k\otimes m_k\in \mathfrak{a}'\otimes_AM$ is likewise sent to $\sum_k a_km_k=0$, so by assumption $z'=0$; since $z$ is the image under $\mathfrak{a}'\otimes_AM \rightarrow \mathfrak{a}\otimes_AM$ of $z'$, we have $z=0$. Now, taking the following short exact sequence

$$0 \rightarrow L \rightarrow N \rightarrow N/L \rightarrow 0$$

and applying the $\Tor$ long exact sequence, we obtain

$$\cdots \rightarrow \Tor_1^A(N/L, M) \rightarrow L\otimes_AM \rightarrow N\otimes_AM \rightarrow (N/L)\otimes_AM \rightarrow 0$$

where $\Tor_1^A(N/L,M)=\Tor_1^A(A/\mathfrak{a},M)$ is $0$, so we obtain the desired result.
:::

From this, we obtain the following corollaries in certain special cases.

::: Corollary 2
Let a field $\mathbb{K}$, a ring $A=\mathbb{K}[t]/(t^2)$, and an $A$-module $M$ be given. Then $M$ is a flat $A$-module if and only if the multiplication $\times t: M \rightarrow tM$ induces an isomorphism $M/tM \rightarrow tM$.
:::
::: Proof
Since the only ideal of $A$ other than $0$ and $A$ is $(t)$, $M$ being flat is equivalent to $(t)\otimes_A M \rightarrow M$ being injective. Meanwhile, $\times t: A \rightarrow (t)$ is an $A$-linear map, and its kernel is $(t)$. More explicitly, this $A$-linear isomorphism is given by

$$A/(t)\cong \mathbb{K} \rightarrow (t);\qquad a+(t)\mapsto at$$

Now from this we obtain the isomorphism

$$M/tM\cong A/(t)\otimes_A M \cong (t)\otimes_A M$$

Meanwhile, the multiplication map $(t)\otimes_AM \rightarrow M$ is obtained through the $A$-bilinear map

$$(t)\times M \rightarrow M;\qquad (ta, x)\mapsto (ta)x$$

and composing this with the above isomorphism, the resulting map $M/tM \rightarrow M$ is given, for any $x+tM\in M/tM$, by

$$x+tM \mapsto (1+(t))\otimes x\mapsto t\otimes x\mapsto tx$$

That is, $M/tM \rightarrow M$ is precisely the map obtained by multiplying by $t$, and since its image is evidently $tM$, $\times t: M/tM \rightarrow tM$ being an isomorphism is equivalent to $(t)\otimes_AM \rightarrow M$ being injective, which in turn is equivalent to $M$ being flat by [Proposition 1](#prop1){: data-lid="in1cb" }.
:::

::: Corollary 3
Fix an element $a\in A$ that is not a zero-divisor. If $M$ is a flat $A$-module, then $a$ is not a zero-divisor on $M$. If $A$ is a PID, the converse also holds.
:::
::: Proof
If $M$ is a flat $A$-module, then $(a)\otimes_AM \rightarrow M$ is an injection, so $a$ is a non-zerodivisor on $M$. Meanwhile, if $A$ is a PID, every ideal of $A$ other than $0$ is generated by a single non-zerodivisor, and in particular, for every ideal $\mathfrak{a}$, $\mathfrak{a}\otimes_AM \rightarrow M$ is injective.
:::

In general, there is no unique way to express an element of a tensor product. The following lemma partially remedies this.

::: Lemma 4
Fix two $A$-modules $M,N$, and suppose $N$ is generated by $\{y_j\}_{j\in J}$. Now, when an arbitrary element of $M\otimes_AN$ is written as a finite sum

$$\sum_{j\in J} x_j\otimes y_j$$

this element is $0$ if and only if there exist a suitable finite index set $I$, elements of $M$, $\{x_i'\}_{i\in I}$, and $a_{ij}\in A$ such that the following equations

$$\sum_{i\in I} a_{ij}x_i'=x_j\quad\text{for all $j$},\qquad \sum_{j\in J} a_{ij}y_j=0\quad\text{for all $i$}$$

hold.
:::
::: Proof
First, if $x_j'$ and $a_{ij}$ satisfying the given conditions all exist, then

$$\sum_{j\in J} x_j\otimes y_j=\sum_{j\in J}\left(\left(\sum_{i\in I}a_{ij}x_i'\right)\otimes y_j\right)=\sum_{i\in I} x_i'\otimes\left(\sum_{j\in J} a_{ij} y_j\right)=0$$

so the reverse direction is easily obtained.

Now, to show the forward direction, let us first consider the case where $N$ is a free module and $\{y_j\}_{j\in J}$ is a basis of $N$. Then by [\[Algebraic Structures\] §Direct Products, Direct Sums, and Tensor Products of Modules, ⁋Theorem 6](/en/math/algebraic_structures/operations_of_modules#thm6){: data-lid="m6qn6" }, $\otimes$ commutes with colimits, and in particular with direct sums, so we obtain the isomorphism

$$M\otimes_AN\cong\bigoplus_{j\in J} (M\otimes_A Ay_j)\cong \bigoplus_{j\in J} M$$

Considering this, the element $\sum_j x_j\otimes y_j$ corresponds to $(x_j)_{j\in J}$, so this element being $0$ is equivalent to all $x_j$ being $0$.

Now, for an arbitrary module $N$, we can choose a suitable free $A$-module $F$ and $\varepsilon: F \rightarrow N$ such that the basis of $F$, $\{f_j\}_{j\in J}$, is sent via $\varepsilon$ to $\{y_j\}_{j\in J}$. ([\[Multilinear Algebra\] §Bases, ⁋Definition 1](/en/math/multilinear_algebra/basis_of_free_modules#def1){: data-lid="f6scz" }) Then we obtain the short exact sequence

$$0 \longrightarrow\ker\varepsilon \longrightarrow F \overset{\varepsilon}{\longrightarrow} N \longrightarrow 0$$

and, again regarding $\ker \varepsilon$ as a quotient of a free module $G$ via [\[Multilinear Algebra\] §Bases, ⁋Proposition 2](/en/math/multilinear_algebra/basis_of_free_modules#prop2){: data-lid="m1j6b" }, we obtain the exact sequence

$$G \rightarrow \ker\varepsilon \rightarrow 0$$

From these we obtain a *free presentation* of $N$

$$G \overset{\eta}{\longrightarrow} F \overset{\varepsilon}{\longrightarrow} N \longrightarrow 0$$

Meanwhile, since $M\otimes_A-$ is right exact, this yields the exact sequence

$$M\otimes_A G \overset{\id_M\otimes\eta}{\longrightarrow} M\otimes_AF \overset{\id_M\otimes \varepsilon}{\longrightarrow} M\otimes_AN \longrightarrow 0$$

and by assumption, $\sum_{j\in J} x_j\otimes f_j$ is sent via $\id_M\otimes\varepsilon$ to $0$. Therefore, from exactness at $M\otimes_AF$, we can choose suitable $x_i'\in M$ and $z_i\in G$ so that

$$\sum_{i\in I} x_i'\otimes\eta(z_i)=(\id_M\otimes\eta)\left(\sum_i x_i'\otimes z_i\right)=\sum_j x_j\otimes f_j$$

Meanwhile, using the basis of $F$, $\{f_j\}_{j\in J}$, we can write

$$\eta(z_i)=\sum_{j\in J} a_{ij}f_j,\qquad\text{$(a_{ij})_{j\in J}$ finitely supported for all $i$}$$

Substituting this into the above equation, from

$$\sum_{j\in J} x_j\otimes f_j=\sum_{i\in I} x_i'\otimes\eta(z_i)=\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J}a_{ij}x_i'\otimes f_j$$

we obtain

$$0=\sum_{j\in J} x_j \otimes f_j-\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J} a_{ij}x_i'\otimes f_j=\sum_{j\in J} \left(x_j-\sum_{i\in I} a_{ij}x_i'\right)\otimes f_j$$

and therefore, by the result shown above for the case of a free module, we know that $x_j=\sum_{i\in I} a_{ij}x_i'$. Now, considering the image of $\eta(z_i)$ under $\varepsilon$,

$$0=(\varepsilon\circ\eta)(z_i)=\varepsilon\left(\sum_{j\in J} a_{ij}f_j\right)=\sum_{j\in J} a_{ij}y_j$$

so we obtain the desired result.
:::

More generally, the following holds.

::: Corollary 5
An $A$-module $M$ is flat if and only if the following condition holds.

> Whenever $x_i\in M$ and $a_i\in A$ satisfying $0=\sum_{i\in I} a_ix_i$ are given, there exist an index set $J$, $x_j'\in M$, and $b_{ij}\in A$ such that the following two equations hold:
>
> $$\sum_{j\in J} b_{ij} x_j'=x_i\quad\text{for all $i$},\qquad \sum_{i\in I} b_{ij} a_i=0\quad\text{for all $j$}$$
:::
::: Proof
By the result of [Proposition 1](#prop1){: data-lid="czilw" }, $M$ being flat is equivalent to the multiplication map

$$\mathfrak{a}\otimes_A M \rightarrow M$$

being injective for every finitely generated ideal $\mathfrak{a}$. This is equivalent to saying that if

$$\sum_i a_i\otimes x_i\in \mathfrak{a}\otimes_AM$$

belongs to the kernel of the above multiplication map, then this element must necessarily be $0$; now, examining when this element is $0$ using [Lemma 4](#lem4){: data-lid="vkosd" } yields the desired result.
:::

Meanwhile, flatness can also be formulated in the language of diagrams, in a form similar to [\[Homological Algebra\] §Resolutions, ⁋Definition 1](/en/math/homological_algebra/resolutions#def1){: data-lid="yjr5i" }.

::: Corollary 6
For an $A$-module $M$, the following two conditions are equivalent.

1. $M$ is a flat $A$-module.
2. For every finitely generated free module $F$, morphism $u:F \rightarrow M$, and monogeneous submodule of $\ker u$, $K$, there exists a diagram
    
    {% diagram Math/Commutative_Algebra/Flatness-1.svg width="5.61em" alt="flatness" %}
    
    such that $G$ is a finitely generated free $A$-module and $K\subseteq \ker v$. 

Moreover, the second condition also holds if $K$ is taken to be an arbitrary finitely generated submodule of $\ker u$. 
:::
::: Proof
The equivalence of the first and second conditions is a diagrammatic transcription of the condition of [Corollary 5](#cor5){: data-lid="ww94d" }. First, suppose $M$ is flat, and let a finitely generated free module $F$ with basis $\{e_i\}_{i\in I}$, a morphism $u:F \rightarrow M$, and a monogeneous submodule of $\ker u$, $K=(\sum_{i\in I} a_ie_i)$, be given. Setting $x_i=u(e_i)$, we have $\sum_{i\in I} a_ix_i=u\left(\sum_{i\in I} a_ie_i\right)=0$, so from [Corollary 5](#cor5){: data-lid="9zvra" } we obtain $x_j'\in M$ and $b_{ij}\in A$ such that $x_i=\sum_{j\in J} b_{ij}x_j'$ and $\sum_{i\in I} b_{ij}a_i=0$ hold; the $J$ here is finite, since it comes from the finite index set of [Lemma 4](#lem4){: data-lid="0gknq" }. Now let the basis of $G=A^J$ be $\{f_j\}_{j\in J}$, and define $v:F \rightarrow G$ by $v(e_i)=\sum_{j\in J} b_{ij}f_j$ and $G \rightarrow M$ by $f_j\mapsto x_j'$; then $e_i$ is carried to $x_i=u(e_i)$, so the above diagram commutes, and since $v\left(\sum_{i\in I} a_ie_i\right)=\sum_{j\in J}\left(\sum_{i\in I} b_{ij}a_i\right)f_j=0$, we have $K\subseteq \ker v$. 

Conversely, assume the second condition, and suppose that $\sum_{i\in I} a_ix_i=0$ holds for given $x_i\in M$ and $a_i\in A$; since this sum is finite, we may take $I$ to be finite, and thus we can apply the second condition to $F=A^I$, with $u(e_i)=x_i$ defining $u:F \rightarrow M$, and to $K=(\sum_{i\in I} a_ie_i)\subseteq \ker u$. For the resulting $v:F \rightarrow G$, choose for $G$ a basis $\{f_j\}_{j\in J}$, write $v(e_i)=\sum_{j\in J} b_{ij}f_j$, and let $x_j'$ be the image of $f_j$ under $G \rightarrow M$; then from the commutativity of the diagram we obtain $x_i=\sum_{j\in J} b_{ij}x_j'$, and from $K\subseteq \ker v$ we obtain $\sum_{i\in I} b_{ij}a_i=0$, so by [Corollary 5](#cor5){: data-lid="1v8cr" }, $M$ is flat. 

Finally, the case where $K$ is a finitely generated submodule of $\ker u$ is obtained by repeatedly applying the second condition. Letting the generators of $K$ be $x_1,\ldots, x_n$, first, to kill the monogeneous submodule $(x_1)$, choose $v_1:F \rightarrow G_1$; then, applying the same process to $G_1 \rightarrow M$ and the monogeneous submodule of $\ker(G_1 \rightarrow M)$, $(v_1(x_2))$, and repeating this up through $x_n$, the composite $v_n\circ\cdots\circ v_1$ is the desired morphism. 
:::

If $M$ is finitely presented, then there exists an exact sequence

$$0 \rightarrow K \rightarrow F \rightarrow M \rightarrow 0$$

Here $F$ is a finitely generated free $A$-module, and $K$ is also finitely generated. If $M$ is flat, then in the above corollary, $\im v\subseteq G$ is carried isomorphically by $G \rightarrow M$ onto $M$. Therefore $G \rightarrow M$ splits, and from this we know that $M$ is a direct summand of $G$. In other words, a finitely presented flat module is the same as a finitely presented projective module. ([\[Multilinear Algebra\] §Projective, Injective, and Flat Modules, ⁋Proposition 4](/en/math/multilinear_algebra/various_modules#prop4){: data-lid="0xwda" })

::: Corollary 7
Fix a field $\mathbb{K}$ and $A=\mathbb{K}[\x]$, and let $E$ be a Noetherian ring that is a flat $A$-algebra. If $E/\x E$ is a domain, and $S$ is the set of elements that, for $f\in E$, are of the form $1-\x f$, then $S^{-1}E$ is a domain.
:::
::: Proof
First, let us reduce the given statement to a simpler form. Since taking localization preserves $\otimes$, replacing $E$ by $S^{-1}E$ still leaves $S^{-1}E$ a flat $A$-algebra, and by [§Localization, ⁋Corollary 9](/en/math/commutative_algebra/localization#cor9){: data-lid="wqcr0" }, $S^{-1}E$ is also Noetherian. Moreover, if $E/\x E$ is a domain, then $S^{-1} E/ \x S^{-1}E$ is also a domain, so we may take $E$ to be $S^{-1}E$ from the start. The choice of $S$ as in the condition can then be replaced by the assumption that every element of $E$ of the form $1-\x f$ is a unit.

Now, under the above condition, suppose ideals of $E$, $\mathfrak{a}, \mathfrak{b}$, satisfy $\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}=0$; we must show that $\mathfrak{a}=0$ or $\mathfrak{b}=0$. Then, since $\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}=0$, enlarging $\mathfrak{a}$ and $\mathfrak{b}$ if necessary, we may assume they are each other's annihilators. Now $\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}=0$ modulo $\x$, and since $E/\x E$ is a domain, we may assume $\mathfrak{b}\subseteq (\x)$. Then $\x$ is a non-zerodivisor of $E$ and $\mathfrak{a}(\mathfrak{b}:(\x))\x=0$, so $(\mathfrak{b}:(\x))$ annihilates $\mathfrak{a}$. That is, $(\mathfrak{b}:(\x))\subseteq \mathfrak{b}$, and hence $\mathfrak{b}=\x\mathfrak{b}$. Meanwhile, since $E$ is Noetherian, $\mathfrak{b}$ is finitely generated, and since for the ideal $(\x)\subseteq E$ we have $(\x)\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}$, by [§Integral Extensions, ⁋Lemma 7](/en/math/commutative_algebra/integral_extension#lem7){: data-lid="dy1zq" } there exists a suitable $f\in E$ such that $(1-\x f)\mathfrak{b}=0$. But by the reduction above, $1-\x f$ is a unit, so $\mathfrak{b}=0$, and we obtain the desired result.
:::
